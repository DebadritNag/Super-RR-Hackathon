# AI pipeline verification (Phase 8, independent audit)

Status label: **EXPERIMENTAL_NOT_VALIDATED**. This document replaces the Phase 7 verification, which
audited a statistical baseline and found no trained model. The old file is kept unchanged as
`ai_pipeline_verification_phase7_aborted.md` (its two CSVs are kept as
`experimental_ai_top10_*_phase7_aborted.csv`). Nothing in the old file was trusted; every check below was re-run.

**Technical success is not reliability.** The models below train, validate and predict end to end.
That shows the code works. It does not show that the player order they produce is repeatable or
that it measures pressure performance. The reliability evidence in section 6 is weak.

Generated by `scripts/phase8_ai_verify.py` (record: `phase8_ai_verification.json`). Test coverage:
`tests/test_phase8_ai_pipeline.py`, `tests/test_phase8_api.py`.

## 1. Does a real trained model exist?

Yes. Nine versioned artefacts exist in `backend/data/models/` (joblib plus a metadata JSON each):
eight gradient-boosting expected-outcome models (`p8_expected_{batter,bowler}_{runs,wicket,dot,boundary}_v1`)
and one win-probability bundle (`p8_wp_v1`: W1 innings-1, W2 chase, and a next-ball class model).
Every metadata file records algorithm, seed (20260512), `trained_on = TRAIN`, features, parameters and
validation metrics. The SHA-256 of each artefact is in `phase8_ai_verification.json`.

| Artefact group | Algorithm | Trained on | Rows (train / validation) |
|---|---|---|---|
| expected batter/bowler runs | scikit-learn gradient boosting regressor | TRAIN (to 2024-12-31) | about 110,700 to 115,100 / 41,700 to 43,300 |
| expected wicket, dot, boundary (6 models) | HistGradientBoosting classifier | TRAIN | about 110,300 to 110,800 / 41,500 to 41,700 |

## 4. Is validation genuine?

Selection used the VALIDATION period once (a 2 x 2 x 2 grid); the TEST period was not opened.
Stored metrics against a constant and a lookup baseline (VALIDATION, out of sample):

| Target | Gain over constant baseline |
|---|---|
| batter runs (RMSE) | 1.63% |
| batter wicket / dot / boundary (log loss) | 1.74% / 2.95% / 3.08% |
| bowler runs (RMSE) | 1.51% |
| bowler wicket / dot / boundary (log loss) | 1.51% / 2.96% / 3.12% |

The gains are real but small. Calibration slopes of the classifiers are 0.94 to 0.98.

Win probability, re-scored from the stored artefact on 20,040 VALIDATION chase deliveries:
Brier 0.1167 against 0.2506 for a constant (the same value as the stored metric), calibration slope 1.13.
The decile table in `p8_wp_calibration` shows the model slightly over-predicts in deciles 3 to 5 and
under-predicts in deciles 8 and 9. W1 (innings 1) Brier gain over constant is 14.3%, slope 0.89;
both WP gates passed.

## 5. Does prediction use the artefact?

Yes. `experimental_scores` obtains every expectation from `expected_from_artefact`, which loads the
joblib file and calls the stored predictor. Tests show that replacing the predictor object changes
the output and that reloading gives a fresh object. The CSVs carry the SHA-256 of the runs and wicket artefacts they
used, and a test recomputes those hashes from disk.

## 6. Is the target appropriate, and is the output reliable?

Target: for each player, the mean of (actual runs minus artefact-expected runs) per pressure delivery
(batters), or (expected minus actual runs charged) per pressure delivery (bowlers), on VALIDATION
(2025) deliveries with pressure intensity P4 at least 0.5. This is a sensible target for "better
than the situation predicts", but:

* Pressure definition P4 did not pass the predeclared validity criteria; it is the best available, not an approved one.
* The score is not centred on the league mean and has no shrinkage. Players with few balls swing widely.
* One calendar year gives 8 to 21 pressure matches per listed player. Intervals use match clusters.
* Split-half reliability by match parity among eligible players: batters r = 0.112 (89 players, Spearman-Brown 0.20); bowlers r = 0.115 (90 players, Spearman-Brown 0.21). Both are far below the predeclared 0.5. The order is mostly noise.
* Selecting the top ten of about 90 players overstates the leaders (winner's curse). Only 3 of 10 batter intervals and 9 of 10 bowler intervals exclude zero, and the zero point is not a league average.

**Conclusion.** The pipeline is technically sound and reproducible. The experimental lists are outputs
of a real model, but they are not evidence of who is best under pressure. They are not the submitted
ranking and are never presented as validated.

## 9. Limits of this audit

Two of eight expected-outcome models were refit (one regressor, one classifier); the other six were
checked for existence, metadata and loadability only. The win-probability bundle was re-scored, not refit.
The audit does not establish that any model improves the pressure ranking; the comparison of approaches
and its reliability results are in `methodology_comparison.md`.
